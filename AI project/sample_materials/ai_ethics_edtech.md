Artificial Intelligence Ethics & Inclusive Educational Systems

As generative artificial intelligence transforms global learning, ensuring equitable access, algorithmic transparency, and bias mitigation is paramount to preserving human agency.

Algorithmic Bias: Machine learning models inherit and amplify systematic historical biases present in training corpora. In education, uncalibrated automated grading or admissions models can disproportionately disadvantage marginalized groups.
Explainable AI (XAI): Refers to methods and techniques that produce human-interpretable explanations for model predictions, such as SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME.
Differential Privacy: Mathematical framework ensuring that the inclusion or exclusion of any individual record in a dataset cannot be determined from aggregate query outputs, protecting student data privacy.
Universal Design for Learning (UDL): Educational framework providing multiple means of representation, engagement, and expression, ensuring neurodivergent and disabled learners have equal access.
Hallucination Detection: Mechanistic interpretability techniques that detect factual divergence in large language models before presenting educational explanations to students.

SDG 4.5 emphasizes eliminating gender and socioeconomic disparities in education through human-centric, verifiable AI.
